Participant Feedback

What participants said

Open-ended feedback collected from participants after completing their tasks — capturing both positive highlights and areas identified for improvement.

Most Liked
01

Faster and more convenient reservation process — participants appreciated how CourtSched significantly reduced the time needed to book a court compared to traditional methods.

Positive
02

The system helps avoid scheduling bias — participants valued the transparency in the scheduling process, noting that the digital system removes subjective favoritism.

Positive
03

Calendar feature for checking schedule — the calendar view was widely praised as intuitive and effective for visualizing court availability at a glance.

Positive
04

Clean and minimalistic graphics — participants responded positively to the visual design, noting that the minimal interface reduced cognitive load and felt easy to navigate.

Positive
05

Overall the system is easy to use and helps organize schedules more efficiently, reducing confusion around double-bookings and time conflicts.

Positive
Least Liked & Issues Identified
01

Confusing button placements — participants reported that some interactive elements were difficult to locate, leading to hesitation during task completion and slightly longer completion times.

High Priority
02

Slow response time and minor delays — some participants noted that the system felt sluggish during page transitions, which negatively impacted the perceived performance of the prototype.

High Priority
03

Time slot buttons are too small for users with larger fingers — this accessibility concern was particularly noted in the court reservation task, where small tap targets caused errors.

High Priority
04

Lack of proper guidance and feedback messages — users felt unsure whether their actions were successful, as the system lacked clear confirmation dialogs or status indicators after key actions.

Medium Priority
05

Accessibility settings not noticeable — several participants struggled to locate the accessibility settings panel, indicating that this feature needs better discoverability through improved placement or visual cues.

Medium Priority
06

The color palette of the application received mixed feedback — some participants found the color choices inconsistent or difficult to read, suggesting a need for improved color contrast and coherence.

// 02.05
Discussion

Discussion of Findings

Finding 01
Strong Core Performance

The CourtSched prototype demonstrated strong baseline usability with a 100% task completion rate and an average SUS score of 79.63 (Grade B — Good). All 40 participants successfully completed their assigned tasks within the designated time limits, confirming that the prototype's core functionality is accessible and learnable.

Finding 02
Viewing Tasks Excel

Tasks focused on viewing information — View Schedule (4.7/5), View Request (4.7/5), and View Notification (4.8/5) — consistently received the highest satisfaction scores. This suggests that the system's information display architecture is well-designed, but interactive elements such as court reservation forms require further refinement.

Finding 03
Interaction Gaps

The lowest-rated task was Court Reservation (4.3/5) — the most complex workflow — where participants reported confusion around button placements and small tap targets. This aligns with Nielsen's heuristics on error prevention and help & documentation, suggesting that the reservation form needs stronger affordance signals and clearer visual hierarchy.

Finding 04
Accessibility Gaps

The accessibility settings task (4.4/5) revealed that while users could eventually find and adjust the settings, the feature lacked visibility. Combined with feedback about small button sizes and limited language support, this indicates the prototype needs a more inclusive design approach targeting a broader and more diverse user base.

"Overall, users were satisfied but recommended improvements to make the system more user-friendly and inclusive — particularly for first-time users, elderly individuals, and those who are not proficient in the default language."

— Summarized Usability Test, CourtSched Group 7
