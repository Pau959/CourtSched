Data Gathering

All instruments, consent forms, respondent profiles, and documentation used during the usability testing of the CourtSched prototype with 40 participants.

// 01.01
Consent Form

Participant Consent Form

All participants were provided with this consent form and voluntarily agreed to participate before any testing commenced.

Partido State University
Court Scheduling System
Usability Testing Study — Informed Consent

You are invited to participate in a usability testing study for the Court Scheduling System, a prototype designed to improve the process of reserving courts for events, sports, and community activities. The purpose of this study is to evaluate the usability of the system — specifically how easy, efficient, and user-friendly it is for users when performing scheduling tasks.

Procedure

Participants will use the CourtSched prototype and perform basic scheduling tasks such as booking a court or checking availability. After completing the tasks, participants will answer a System Usability Scale (SUS) questionnaire based on their experience.

Duration & Participation

The entire activity will take approximately 1-3 minutes to complete. Participation is completely voluntary. Participants may choose not to participate or withdraw at any time without penalty.

Confidentiality

All information collected will be treated with strict confidentiality. Participant identities will not be disclosed, and all responses will be used solely for academic purposes.

Risks & Benefits

This study involves minimal risk, limited only to the time required to complete the activity. While there are no direct personal benefits, participant feedback will help improve the system and contribute to a more user-friendly scheduling process.

Survey Instruments

System Usability Scale (SUS) Questionnaire

Participants rated each of the 10 SUS statements on a 5-point Likert scale (1 = Strongly Disagree, 5 = Strongly Agree) after completing the testing tasks.

# Statement Type Scale
Q1 I think that I would like to use this system frequently. Positive 1 – 5
Q2 I found the system unnecessarily complex. Negative 1 – 5
Q3 I thought the system was easy to use. Positive 1 – 5
Q4 I think that I would need the support of a technical person to use this system. Negative 1 – 5
Q5 I found the various functions in this system were well integrated. Positive 1 – 5
Q6 I thought there was too much inconsistency in this system. Negative 1 – 5
Q7 I would imagine that most people would learn to use this system very quickly. Positive 1 – 5
Q8 I found the system very cumbersome to use. Negative 1 – 5
Q9 I felt very confident using the system. Positive 1 – 5
Q10 I needed to learn a lot of things before I could get going with this system. Negative 1 – 5
SUS Scoring Formula

For odd-numbered questions (positive): subtract 1 from the user's score. For even-numbered questions (negative): subtract the user's score from 5. Sum all adjusted scores and multiply by 2.5 to obtain the final SUS score (0–100).

// 01.04
Open-Ended Questions

Qualitative Feedback Instruments

In addition to the SUS scale, participants answered two open-ended questions to capture richer qualitative data about their experience with the system.

Question 01
Improvements & Suggestions

"What improvements or suggestions can you recommend to enhance the usability of the system?"

This question gathered forward-looking suggestions from users — identifying what features, interactions, or design changes would make the system more effective and inclusive for all user types.

Question 02
Most & Least Liked

"What did you like most and least about your experience using the system?"

This question captured immediate emotional reactions — both positive highlights and pain points — allowing the team to understand what aspects of the interface resonated most strongly with users.
